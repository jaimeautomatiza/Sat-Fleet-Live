What is a TLE, and how do you read one?

A TLE, or two-line element set, is two lines of text, 69 characters each, that describe a satellite's orbit at one moment. Give it to the SGP4 model and you get the satellite's position at any time near that moment. Here is today's ISS TLE, decoded column by column, plus what changed in July 2026 when the satellite catalogue ran out of five-digit numbers.

The short answer

A TLE (two-line element set) is a compact, fixed-width text format that lists the orbital elements of an Earth-orbiting object at a specific time, called the epoch. Every column has a fixed meaning: line 1 identifies the object and describes drag, line 2 describes the size, shape and orientation of the orbit and where the object was along it. The values are mean elements fitted for the SGP4 and SDP4 models, so a TLE is only accurate when it is propagated with one of those models.

TLE at a glance

Two-line element set. With a name line on top it is called a three-line element set (3LE).

Works with

Decoding the ISS TLE

Read in plain language, the ISS element set above says this:

  • It was valid at 11:57:28 UTC on 5 October 2026Day 278 of 2026 is 5 October, and 0.49823669 of a day is 11 hours 57 minutes 28 seconds.
  • It circles Earth every 92.98 minutes1,440 minutes in a day divided by 15.48742155 orbits per day.
  • It flies about 416 to 425 km upMean motion gives the orbit size (semi-major axis about 6,799 km); eccentricity 0.0006877 makes it almost circular.
  • It passes over latitudes up to 51.6° north and southThe inclination sets how far from the equator the ground track reaches.
  • It was the 67th launch of 1998Designator 98067A: the Zarya module, the first piece of the station.
  • It is losing height slowlyA positive first derivative of mean motion means drag is shrinking the orbit, so the station speeds up.

Two fields are easy to misread. The last four characters of line 1, 9992, are the element set number 999 followed by the checksum 2, not one number. And the last six characters of line 2, 588859, are the revolution number 58885 plus the checksum 9. Because that counter rolls over after 99999, the ISS shows 58,885 even though it had flown about 158,900 orbits by October 2026.

Implied decimals, exponents and the checksum

The format saves space with a few conventions that trip up anyone parsing TLEs by hand:

  • Implied decimal point. Eccentricity is written without 0., so 0006877 is 0.0006877.
  • Packed exponent. The second derivative and BSTAR use a five-digit mantissa with an implied leading decimal point, then the sign and power of ten. 10196-3 means +0.10196 × 10-3, and 00000+0 (or 00000-0, depending on the provider) means zero.
  • Halved and sixth derivatives. The two mean-motion derivative fields are already divided by 2 and by 6. SGP4 does not use them at all; they are left over from older models. Drag in SGP4 comes from BSTAR.
  • Two-digit years. 57 to 99 are 1957 to 1999, and 00 to 56 are 2000 to 2056, so the format runs out of years in 2057.

How the checksum works

Each line ends with a check digit. Add up every digit in the first 68 characters, count each minus sign as 1, ignore letters, spaces, full stops and plus signs, and keep the last digit of the total. For the ISS line 1 above the digits add up to 182, so the checksum is 2. A wrong check digit almost always means the line was cut, wrapped or edited by hand.

How old is too old? Epoch and accuracy

A TLE is a snapshot. It is most accurate at its epoch and gets worse the further you propagate from it, forwards or backwards. For a satellite in low Earth orbit, a fresh element set typically places it within about a kilometre or two, and the error grows by a few kilometres for every day of propagation. It grows faster when the upper atmosphere swells during geomagnetic storms, and an element set is simply wrong after a manoeuvre until a new one is published. The ISS, for example, raises its orbit with reboosts.

Higher orbits are calmer. GPS and geostationary satellites feel almost no drag, so their element sets stay usable for much longer. As a rule of thumb, a TLE a day or two old is fine for watching a low satellite pass overhead; one a week old will put the ISS noticeably early or late. TLEs are good enough for tracking and pass predictions, but they are not precise enough to judge collision risk on their own.

What this means for spotting satellites

Predictions more than two or three days ahead are estimates. Check a pass or a transit again the day before, when a newer element set is out.

From TLE to position: SGP4 and SDP4

The elements in a TLE are not the textbook Keplerian elements of the orbit at that instant. They are mean elements, fitted so that the SGP4 model (Simplified General Perturbations 4) reproduces the observed motion. SGP4 accounts for Earth's flattening and for atmospheric drag through BSTAR. Objects whose orbit takes 225 minutes or longer switch to the SDP4 branch, which adds the pull of the Moon and Sun and resonance effects.

SGP4 returns a position and velocity in a frame called TEME (true equator, mean equinox). A tracker then rotates it into Earth-fixed coordinates to get latitude, longitude and altitude, and from there the direction to look from your location. Every position on the SatFleet Live map goes through these steps, recalculated continuously in your browser.

Because the elements only make sense inside SGP4, feeding them into a simple Kepler calculator gives errors that grow quickly. Well-tested SGP4 implementations exist for most languages, including satellite.js for JavaScript and python-sgp4 and Skyfield for Python.

2026: the catalogue outgrew the TLE

The TLE has five columns for the catalogue number, and that limit arrived in 2026. Numbers 70000 to 99999 are set aside for temporary uses, such as analyst objects, so permanent numbers stopped at 69999. On 11 July 2026 the US Space Force catalogue gave number 100000 to Saramago, a Portuguese CubeSat launched on 30 March 2026 on the Transporter-16 rideshare (designator 2026-067CY). Every object catalogued since then has a six-digit number.

CelesTrak, the most widely used free source, does not publish classic TLEs for objects numbered 100000 or more. It has offered the same data in the OMM format since May 2020, and its legacy fixed-width catalogue file also stops below 70000. Software that only reads TLE text quietly misses every new satellite, which is why many trackers moved to OMM in 2026.

Alpha-5: the stopgap

Alpha-5 squeezes larger numbers into the same five columns by replacing the first two digits with a letter: A stands for 10, B for 11 and so on up to Z for 33, skipping I and O so they are not confused with 1 and 0. Saramago's 100000 becomes A0000, and the scheme tops out at Z9999, which is 339999. An Alpha-5 line looks like this: 

SARAMAGO
1 A0000U 26067CY  26228.87294806  .00005425  00000-0  24826-3 0  9996
2 A0000  97.4630 186.7381 0008890 142.7306 217.4548 15.20903303 20923

Alpha-5 only buys time, and not every program or provider understands it. The long-term answer is OMM.

TLE vs OMM

OMM (Orbit Mean-Elements Message) is an international standard from CCSDS, the consultative committee for space data systems. It carries exactly the same SGP4 mean elements as a TLE, but every value has a name, so nothing depends on column positions.

Where to get TLE data

  • Space-Track.org: the official public catalogue, run for the US Space Force. Free, but you need an account, must accept its user agreement and must stay within its query limits. It serves TLE, 3LE and OMM.
  • CelesTrak: a non-profit run by Dr T.S. Kelso that republishes the data with no account, grouped into handy lists (space stations, active satellites, Starlink, GPS, weather). It offers TLE for five-digit objects and OMM for everything. Respect its usage policy and do not download the same file more often than it changes.
  • Per-satellite pages: N2YO, SatNOGS and the SatFleet Live satellite pages show the current elements for one object, which is handy for a quick look.

How SatFleet Live uses orbital elements

  1. Every 2 hours, SatFleet Live's server downloads the latest elements for active satellites from Space-Track, in JSON (OMM) form.
  2. It keeps the active ones. Satellites must be on CelesTrak's active list or have launched in the last 30 days, still be in orbit and have elements less than 30 days old. Objects with incomplete data are skipped rather than drawn in the wrong place. Today that is 16,000+ satellites.
  3. Your browser does the maths. satellite.js runs SGP4 for every satellite on the map, in 2D, 3D and AR, and for Next Passes and the Sun & Moon transit finder.
  4. Once a day, a snapshot is saved as classic TLE text for Playback. Today and yesterday are free; older dates, up to 30 days, are part of Premium.

Because SatFleet Live reads elements as OMM rather than parsing TLE text, the catalogue number is just a number to it, and satellites numbered 100000 and above show up like any other.

Frequently asked questions

What does TLE stand for?

Two-line element set. It is two lines of fixed-width text, 69 characters each, that describe a satellite's orbit at one moment called the epoch. With a name line on top it is sometimes called a 3LE.

How do I read a TLE?

By column position, not by spaces. Line 1 holds the catalogue number, launch designator, epoch and drag terms. Line 2 holds the inclination, right ascension of the ascending node, eccentricity (with an implied leading decimal point), argument of perigee, mean anomaly, mean motion in revolutions per day and the revolution count. The last character of each line is a checksum. The column tables above list every field, and the decoder does it for you.

Where can I get TLE data for free?

Space-Track.org is the official source, run for the US Space Force, and needs a free account. CelesTrak republishes the data without an account, grouped by type, in TLE and in the newer OMM formats. Per-satellite pages on sites such as N2YO and SatNOGS also show the latest TLE.

How accurate is a TLE?

For a low Earth orbit satellite, a fresh TLE typically puts it within about a kilometre or two of its true position. The error grows by a few kilometres for each day you move away from the epoch, faster during geomagnetic storms or after the satellite manoeuvres. TLEs are fine for tracking and pass predictions, not for collision avoidance.

How is the TLE checksum calculated?

Add every digit in the first 68 characters of the line, count each minus sign as 1, ignore letters, spaces, full stops and plus signs, and take the last digit of the total. That digit must match character 69.

Why do some new satellites have no TLE?

The TLE has room for a five-digit catalogue number. The five-digit range ran out on 11 July 2026, when catalogue number 100000 was given to the Portuguese CubeSat Saramago. CelesTrak does not publish classic TLEs for objects numbered 100000 and above; their data is available in the OMM format, which has no such limit.

What is Alpha-5?

A stopgap that squeezes catalogue numbers from 100000 to 339999 into the five-character TLE field by replacing the first two digits with a letter: A means 10, B means 11 and so on to Z for 33, skipping I and O. Catalogue number 100000 becomes A0000. Not every data provider or program supports it.

What is the difference between a TLE and OMM?

They carry the same SGP4 mean elements. A TLE packs them into fixed columns with implied decimals and two-digit years. OMM (CCSDS Orbit Mean-Elements Message) labels every value and comes as XML, JSON, CSV or KVN, uses four-digit years and allows catalogue numbers up to nine digits. CelesTrak has offered OMM since May 2020.

Can I use TLE elements with a normal Kepler orbit calculator?

Not reliably. TLE values are mean elements fitted for the SGP4/SDP4 models, which include drag and Earth's flattening. Plugging them into a simple Keplerian propagator gives position errors that grow quickly. Use an SGP4 library such as satellite.js, python-sgp4 or Skyfield.

Why does the ISS TLE show only about 58,000 orbits?

The revolution number has five columns, so it rolls over after 99999. The ISS has flown well over 150,000 orbits since 1998, but the field only shows the last five digits.

Does SatFleet Live use TLE data?

Yes. SatFleet Live downloads the same SGP4 elements for active satellites from Space-Track every two hours, in JSON (OMM) form, and calculates every position in your browser with satellite.js, a JavaScript implementation of SGP4. Playback keeps one snapshot per day as classic TLE text.
