It runs in any browser with no account, and as an app for iPhone, iPad and Android.

What SatFleet Live is

SatFleet Live is a free, real-time space tracking platform. It began as a satellite tracker and now covers the whole space environment: more than 16,000 active satellites, the debris that surrounds them, predicted re-entries counting down to the minute, rocket launches, the Moon, Mars and the entire solar system.

Every position comes from a real dataset: orbital elements from CelesTrak and NORAD for satellites, Space-Track for debris and re-entries, and NASA/JPL Horizons for the Moon, Mars and deep space. It runs in a web browser with nothing to install and no account to create.

The 3D globe

The Earth, Moon and Mars maps are interactive 3D globes built on CesiumJS, with real imagery on a realistic sphere. Satellites sit at their true altitudes, so a Starlink satellite at about 550 km is visibly closer to the surface than a GPS satellite at about 20,200 km or a geostationary weather satellite at about 35,800 km. Rotate, zoom and tilt freely; selecting a satellite draws its orbit.

Layers and Filters control what the globe shows: switch between Active objects and Space debris, filter by category, and turn the Re-entries and Rockets layers on or off. Deep Space uses a separate engine, because a scale that works a few hundred kilometres up cannot stretch to the billions of kilometres between planets.

Satellite categories and filters

In the Active view every satellite is colour-coded by category, so the map stays readable with thousands of objects on screen. Search jumps straight to a satellite by name, the Layers panel filters by type, and a legend on the map shows what each colour means.

Space debris tracker

Switch Layers and Filters from Active objects to Space debris and the globe replaces working satellites with the fragments and spent rocket bodies that orbit Earth. The two views are separate on purpose: thousands of fragments drawn over 16,000 satellites would make both unreadable.

Debris coloured by origin

Most tracked debris traces back to a handful of events. Each dot takes the colour of its origin, so you can see how a single collision or weapons test spreads along an orbit over the years.

Filter by origin and size

Toggle any origin on or off and filter by size: large, medium or small, the object's radar cross-section class in the public catalogue. Dot size follows the same value, so rocket bodies and large fragments look heavier than small ones.

  1. Open Layers and Filters. Tap Layers in the toolbar.
  2. Choose Space debris. The switch at the top toggles between Active objects and Space debris.
  3. Pick origins and sizes. Show only Fengyun 1C, only rocket bodies or only large objects.
  4. Switch back to Active. Working satellites return in one tap.

Why it matters: in low Earth orbit, debris travels at roughly 7 to 8 kilometres per second, so even small fragments can damage a working spacecraft. Seeing where the clouds concentrate shows why orbital debris is one of the main long-term risks for satellite constellations.

About the debris data

Positions come from Space-Track.org (US 18th Space Defense Squadron). SatFleet Live includes fragments and rocket bodies that have not decayed and have orbital elements from the last 30 days, refreshed every 12 hours. The public catalogue only holds objects large enough to track, roughly 10 cm and up in low Earth orbit. Debris objects are not selectable, and Playback works in the Active view only.

Re-entry tracker

Turn on the Re-entries layer and SatFleet Live plots every object the US space surveillance network currently predicts will fall back into the atmosphere: dead satellites, spent rocket stages and debris. Each appears as a diamond marker, coloured by how soon it is expected to come down, with a shaded zone showing where it could fall.

What a selected re-entry shows

Selecting a marker flies the map to it and opens a panel with a live countdown, the time uncertainty, the object type (satellite, rocket body or debris, with the country when known), the predicted latitude and longitude, and its NORAD ID. A List button opens a searchable, time-sorted list of every upcoming re-entry, with a filter for objects flagged as high interest.

How to read the shaded fall zone

When the timing is tight, the zone is a strip along the object's ground track. When it is not, it becomes a band of latitudes the orbit can reach. Fainter shading means a less precise prediction, and zones with an uncertainty above ±30 minutes are only drawn when you select them.

A prediction is not an impact point

The predicted point is where the object should cross 10 km altitude, not where pieces will land. Uncertainty can span many hours when the event is days away and narrows as it approaches. Most objects burn up, and fragments that survive can land far from the predicted point along the ground track. Predictions come from Space-Track's Tracking and Impact Prediction (TIP) messages, refreshed every six hours.

Playback (Time Travel)

Playback adds a timeline to the map. Choose a date and orbits replay from that moment instead of from now. It works on every map: Earth satellites, Moon orbiters, Mars orbiters, and the planets and spacecraft of the solar system.

  1. Tap Playback. The button sits at the bottom of the map, next to Explore. The panel is called Time Travel.
  2. Pick a date. On Earth, the calendar highlights archived dates. On the Moon, Mars and Deep Space maps, any date in range works.
  3. Press Go. History loads day by day in the background, so you can start watching straight away.
  4. Play or scrub the timeline. Speeds run from real time to hours per second; Deep Space goes up to a full year per second.

Next Passes

Next Passes answers the question every satellite watcher asks: when can I actually see something? Share or enter your location, pick a satellite or let it suggest the ISS and bright Starlink trains, and get upcoming passes with the exact time, maximum elevation, compass direction and visibility.

  1. Set your location. Use GPS or enter coordinates. Nothing is stored without your permission.
  2. Pick a satellite or category. Search by name, or browse the ISS, Starlink and other visible satellites.
  3. Read the pass times. Start time, maximum elevation, direction and expected brightness for each pass.
  4. Go outside and look up. Passes marked visible can be seen with the naked eye around dusk or dawn.

Rocket launches

SatFleet Live follows upcoming and in-progress rocket launches worldwide, including SpaceX Starship and Falcon 9 missions, with countdowns, mission details and launch pad markers on the map. Close to liftoff, a live video stream appears so you can watch without leaving the page. With Premium, Trajectory Preview simulates the complete flight path in 3D, including stage separations, booster landings and payload deployment.

AR Sky View

On supported phones, AR Sky View uses the compass and camera to overlay real satellite positions on the sky above you. Point the phone up and any satellite overhead appears as a marker you can tap for details, a practical way to identify the bright, fast-moving dot you just spotted.

Explore the four maps

The Explore button, a planet icon in the navigation bar on desktop or a floating button at the bottom of the screen on mobile, switches between Earth, the Moon, Mars and Deep Space without losing your place. Getting from a Starlink pass over your city to Voyager 1 in interstellar space takes two taps.

The Moon: landing sites and live orbiters

The Moon map is a real 3D lunar globe rendered from NASA Moon Trek imagery. Every historic landing site is marked and clickable, with the agency, the year and a short history of the mission.

  • Crewed Apollo missions: Apollo 11 to 17, including the missions that carried a Lunar Roving Vehicle.
  • Robotic missions: Luna, Surveyor and Chang'e landers and rovers, including Lunokhod and Yutu.
  • Live lunar orbiters: LRO (NASA), Chandrayaan-2 (ISRO) and Danuri (KARI), positioned with NASA/JPL Horizons.

Tap an orbiter to see its live position and speed and watch its path trace around the Moon. A day-night terminator shows which side is lit, calculated from the real lunar phase.

Mars: rovers, orbiters and mission history

The Mars map is a 3D model of Mars with its true oblate shape, NASA Trek surface imagery and everything humanity has sent there, active and historic.

Curiosity and Perseverance

Both operating rovers are shown at their real position with a trail of every kilometre they have driven, built from NASA's published rover telemetry rather than a simulation. Selecting a rover shows its landing site, mission details and total distance driven.

Mission history back to 1976

The Historic Sites layer adds every earlier landing: Viking 1 and 2 (1976, the first successful Mars landings), Pathfinder (1997, which carried Sojourner, the first Mars rover), Spirit and Opportunity (2004), Phoenix (2008), InSight (2018) and Zhurong (2021, China's first Mars rover), along with Ingenuity, the first aircraft to achieve powered flight on another planet.

Orbiters and the moons of Mars

MRO and Mars Odyssey (NASA), Mars Express (ESA) and Hope (UAE) are tracked live with real orbital speed, and Phobos and Deimos are shown with real photography. Lighting follows the real Martian rotation of about 24 hours 37 minutes.

Deep Space: the solar system, live

Deep Space is a live map of the solar system, from the Sun to interstellar space. Every planet, dwarf planet, major moon and active interplanetary spacecraft is placed with NASA/JPL Horizons ephemeris data, the system professional astronomers use to plan missions. Unlike a static diagram, every dot moves because something really moved.

Planets and dwarf planets

All 8 planets appear in their true current position around the Sun, with the dwarf planets Pluto and Ceres. Real distances are too large for a screen, so the default view compresses distance while keeping every direction and relative position accurate.

Spacecraft, tracked by name

  • Voyager 1 and 2: the most distant human-made objects, in interstellar space more than 24 billion km away and still transmitting after nearly 50 years.
  • James Webb Space Telescope: at its real position near the Sun-Earth L2 point, 1.5 million km from Earth.
  • Parker Solar Probe: the fastest object ever built, diving closer to the Sun than any spacecraft before it.
  • And more: Juno, BepiColombo, New Horizons, Europa Clipper, Lucy, Psyche, Hera and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope.

Moons around their planets

Beyond Earth's Moon, Deep Space tracks Phobos and Deimos at Mars and the four Galilean moons of Jupiter (Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto), each orbiting its real parent planet.

Real Scale View

Real Scale View shows the solar system exactly as it would look from any planet, moon, dwarf planet or spacecraft you select, with every nearby body drawn at its true angular size, calculated from its real radius and real distance.

  • From the Moon, Earth appears as an astronaut on the lunar surface would see it, far larger than the Moon ever looks from Earth.
  • From a spacecraft mid-journey, its home world shrinks to a point of light as it travels.
  • From Jupiter, its four largest moons appear at the size and distance they would have from the cloud tops.

The view projects each object the way a camera lens would, so it answers the question "what would this actually look like from here?" rather than showing an artist's impression.

Distance, speed and light time between any two objects

Every object on the Deep Space map includes a comparison tool. Select one object, search for another, and the result updates instantly from real NASA/JPL positions, not screen positions.

  • Real distance: in kilometres for nearby objects, or in astronomical units with the kilometre equivalent.
  • Relative velocity: how fast the distance between the two objects is changing right now.
  • Light time: how long light, or a radio signal, takes to cross that distance.

Pick Voyager 1 and Earth and you will see that light needs most of a day to make the crossing, the same delay every radio command to Voyager has to travel through.

How it works

SatFleet Live is built on real orbital mechanics and real space agency data. Nothing is simulated where a real dataset exists.

Technology behind each part of SatFleet Live
PartHow it is computed
Earth satellitesThe industry-standard SGP4 propagation model, fed with orbital elements from CelesTrak/NORAD and recalculated continuously in the background.
Debris and re-entriesSpace-Track.org catalogue of the US 18th Space Defense Squadron. Debris refreshes every 12 hours; re-entry predictions every six hours.
Playback on EarthA snapshot of the orbital data is archived every day and propagated with SGP4 to rebuild past positions.
Moon, Mars and Deep SpaceNASA/JPL Horizons ephemeris system, refreshed automatically and queried for any Playback date.
Rover trailsNASA's published waypoint data for Curiosity and Perseverance, refreshed as the rovers move, not interpolated.
Deep Space scaleCompressed distance with true direction for navigation; true angular size and distance in Real Scale View.
RenderingCesiumJS for Earth, the Moon and Mars, with real imagery and true body shapes; a dedicated engine for solar-system distances.

Data sources and accuracy

  • Earth satellites: orbital elements from CelesTrak/NORAD, propagated with SGP4, accurate to within a few kilometres for most objects.
  • Space debris and re-entries: Space-Track.org (US 18th Space Defense Squadron). Debris refreshes every 12 hours and re-entry predictions every six hours; predictions are forecasts with an uncertainty window, not guarantees.
  • Moon, Mars and Deep Space: orbiters, planets, moons, spacecraft and lighting from NASA/JPL Horizons, including for Playback dates.
  • Rocket launches: The Space Devs Launch Library.
  • Rover positions and trails: official NASA rover telemetry, refreshed as Curiosity and Perseverance move.
  • Surface imagery: NASA Trek mosaics for the Moon and Mars; Solar System Scope textures (CC BY 4.0) for Deep Space planets.

SatFleet Live is for information and education. Positions and predictions can contain inaccuracies or delays and must not be used for navigation, safety or other critical decisions.

Frequently asked questions

What is SatFleet Live?

SatFleet Live is a free real-time space tracker. It shows more than 16,000 active satellites on an interactive 3D globe, a separate space debris view, predicted re-entries with countdowns, rocket launches, and Moon, Mars and Deep Space maps built on NASA/JPL Horizons data. Playback replays positions from past dates. It runs in any browser and as an app for Android, iPhone and iPad.

Is SatFleet Live free?

Yes. The live Earth, Moon, Mars and Deep Space maps, satellite search, the space debris view, the re-entry tracker, rocket launches and AR Sky View are free with no account. Playback is free for today and yesterday on Earth. SatFleet Premium costs €2.99 per month after a 7-day free trial and adds older Playback dates, Time Travel on the Moon, Mars and Deep Space maps, Rocket Trajectory Preview, 7-day pass forecasts and pass alerts.

How many satellites does SatFleet Live track?

The Active view shows more than 16,000 operational satellites with current NORAD orbital data, from GPS and the ISS to Starlink, weather and military satellites. Defunct hardware stays out of this view. Space debris has its own view, and together the two cover nearly 30,000 tracked objects.

Can I track space debris on SatFleet Live?

Yes. Open Layers and switch from Active objects to Space debris. Fragments and spent rocket bodies are coloured by origin (Fengyun 1C, Cosmos 2251, Iridium 33, Cosmos 1408, rocket bodies and other debris) and can be filtered by radar cross-section size. The data comes from Space-Track.org and refreshes every 12 hours.

How does the satellite re-entry tracker work?

The Re-entries layer plots objects that Space-Track's Tracking and Impact Prediction (TIP) messages expect to fall back into the atmosphere. Markers are red for under 6 hours, orange for under 24 hours, yellow for under 3 days and grey for more than 3 days, with a shaded possible fall zone. Predictions refresh every six hours.

Is the predicted re-entry point where the satellite will crash?

No. The predicted point is where the object is expected to cross 10 km altitude, not where fragments will land. Most objects burn up, and surviving pieces can land far from the predicted point along the ground track.

What is Playback (Time Travel)?

Playback replays satellites, planets and spacecraft from a past date. Pick a date, press Go, then play or scrub the timeline. Earth satellites use a rolling archive of up to 30 days, with today and yesterday free. Moon orbiters go back to June 2009, and Mars and Deep Space back to October 1957, as Premium features.

Does SatFleet Live track the Moon and Mars?

Yes. The Moon map shows historic landing sites and live lunar orbiters (LRO, Chandrayaan-2, Danuri). The Mars map shows Curiosity and Perseverance with real driving trails from NASA telemetry, historic landers back to Viking in 1976, live orbiters, and Phobos and Deimos.

What is the Deep Space map?

A live map of the solar system with all 8 planets, Pluto and Ceres, major moons, and active spacecraft such as Voyager 1 and 2, the James Webb Space Telescope and Parker Solar Probe, positioned with NASA/JPL Horizons data. It includes Real Scale View and a distance, speed and light-time calculator.

How accurate are the positions on SatFleet Live?

Earth satellites use CelesTrak/NORAD orbital elements with the SGP4 model, accurate to within a few kilometres for most objects. Debris and re-entries come from Space-Track. Moon, Mars and Deep Space positions come from NASA/JPL Horizons, and rover trails from official NASA telemetry.

Can I track the ISS on SatFleet Live?

Yes. The ISS is always on the Earth map with its live altitude (about 400 km), speed (about 27,600 km/h) and position, and Next Passes shows when it will fly over your location.

Is there a SatFleet Live app for iPhone and Android?

Yes. SatFleet Live is free on Google Play for Android and on the App Store for iPhone and iPad, with the same maps as the website plus push notifications. On iPhone it adds an ISS widget for the Home Screen and Lock Screen and launch countdowns as Live Activities in the Dynamic Island.
