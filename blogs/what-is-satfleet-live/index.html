What Is SatFleet Live?

SatFleet Live is a real-time space tracking platform that started as a satellite tracker and has since grown into something bigger: a live map of the Earth, the Moon, and Mars, all built on the same real-time 3D engine. It runs entirely in your browser with no download required, and there's also a free Android app on Google Play for an optimised mobile experience with push notifications.

On Earth, SatFleet Live displays over 16,000 active, operational satellites with current orbital data: GPS and navigation constellations, the International Space Station, China's Tiangong, the entire Starlink fleet, weather satellites, military spacecraft, Earth observation satellites, amateur radio cubesats, and thousands more. Space debris and defunct hardware are deliberately excluded — every dot you see is a functioning piece of hardware in orbit right now.

But the map doesn't stop at Earth orbit. Tap the Explore button and you can jump straight to the Moon — with every historic landing site from Apollo to Chang'e, plus live lunar orbiters — or to Mars, where the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers are tracked live with their real driving trails pulled directly from NASA telemetry, alongside every historic Mars lander since Viking in 1976 and a fleet of active orbiters and natural moons positioned using real NASA/JPL Horizons data.

SatFleet Live also integrates live rocket launch tracking with embedded video streams, a Next Passes predictor that calculates exactly when any satellite will fly over your GPS location, a compass-based AR Sky View for your phone, and detailed per-satellite information pages. Everything updates continuously — Earth satellite positions recalculate every second, and Moon/Mars orbiter and rover data refreshes automatically in the background.

The 3D Globe — Every Orbit, in Perspective

The Earth map is a fully interactive 3D globe powered by CesiumJS with satellite imagery draped over a realistic sphere. Satellites appear as colour-coded dots at their correct altitudes — a Starlink satellite at ~550 km sits visibly closer to the surface than a GPS satellite at ~20,200 km or a geostationary weather satellite at ~35,800 km. You can rotate, zoom, and tilt freely, and orbit paths draw themselves in real time when you select a satellite.

The same 3D engine — CesiumJS, real orbital data, and smooth real-time animation — is exactly what powers the Moon and Mars maps described below. Building all three on one shared engine means the experience feels identical wherever in the solar system you're looking.

Explore — Switching Between Earth, Moon & Mars

Look for the small Explore button — a planet icon in the navigation bar on desktop, or a floating pill at the bottom of the screen on mobile. Tapping it opens a quick switcher to jump straight to whichever world you want to look at next, without losing your place. It's the same button on every map, so getting from a Starlink pass over your city to the Perseverance rover on Mars takes two taps.

Mars: Live Rovers, Orbiters & Mission History

The Mars map is the newest and most ambitious addition to SatFleet Live — a real 3D model of Mars using its true oblate shape, NASA Trek surface imagery, and a full roster of everything humanity has sent there, active and historic.

Curiosity & Perseverance, tracked live

The two rovers currently operating on Mars are shown with their real position and a trail showing every kilometre they've actually driven, sourced directly from NASA's own published rover telemetry — not a simulation. Click either rover to see its landing site, mission details, and total distance driven to date.

Mission history back to 1976

An optional Historic Sites layer adds every prior Mars landing: Viking 1 and 2 (the first successful Mars landings, 1976), Pathfinder (1997, which delivered Sojourner, the first-ever Mars rover), Spirit and Opportunity (2004), Phoenix (2008), InSight (2018), and Zhurong (China's first Mars rover, 2021).

Orbiters and the two moons of Mars

Active orbiters — MRO and Mars Odyssey (NASA), Mars Express (ESA), and Hope/EMM (UAE) — are tracked live with real orbital speed in km/h relative to the surface. Phobos and Deimos, the two natural moons of Mars, are shown too, rendered with real photography rather than plain markers. All positions come from NASA/JPL Horizons, and Mars' own day/night lighting is calculated from its real ~24h 37m rotation.

How It Works: The Technology Behind It

SatFleet Live is built entirely on real orbital mechanics and real space agency data — nothing is simulated or approximated where a real dataset exists.

🌍
Earth satellites — SGP4 + TLE Every satellite's position is computed with the industry-standard SGP4 propagation model, fed by TLE orbital data from CelesTrak/NORAD. Positions recalculate continuously in the background without freezing the page.
🌕
Moon & Mars orbiters — NASA/JPL Horizons Every orbiter, moon, and the Sun's position (for lighting) is queried directly from NASA/JPL's Horizons ephemeris system — the same tool used by planetary scientists — refreshed automatically.
🚙
Rover trails — real NASA telemetry Curiosity and Perseverance's driving trails are built from NASA's own published rover waypoint data, refreshed automatically as each rover moves — not estimated or interpolated.
🖥️
Rendering — CesiumJS All three maps — Earth, Moon and Mars — share the same CesiumJS 3D rendering engine, with real imagery, true body shapes (including Mars' oblateness), and smooth interpolated motion between data updates.

Next Passes — Predict Satellite Overheads

The Next Passes tool answers the question every satellite watcher asks: when can I actually see something? Enter or share your location, pick a satellite (or let it suggest the ISS and bright Starlink trains), and get a list of upcoming passes with exact time, maximum elevation, compass direction, and visibility.

Live Rocket Launches & Countdowns

SatFleet Live tracks upcoming and in-progress rocket launches worldwide, with live countdowns, mission details, and launch pad markers on the map. When a rocket is close to launch, a live video stream appears automatically so you can watch liftoff without leaving the page.

AR Sky View — Satellites on Your Camera

On supported phones, AR Sky View uses your device's compass and camera to overlay real satellite positions directly onto the live view of the sky above you. Point your phone up, and any satellite currently overhead appears as a coloured marker you can tap for details — a genuinely useful way to identify what that bright, fast-moving dot you just spotted actually is.

Data Sources & Accuracy

Earth satellites: TLE orbital data from CelesTrak/NORAD, propagated with SGP4 — accurate to within a few kilometres for most objects.

Moon & Mars orbiters, moons, and lighting: Real ephemeris data from NASA/JPL's Horizons system.

Rover positions & trails: Official NASA rover telemetry, refreshed automatically as Curiosity and Perseverance move.

Surface imagery: NASA Trek mosaics for both the Moon and Mars.
