As of October 5, 2026, there are 14 people in space: 11 on the International Space Station (ISS) and 3 on China's Tiangong space station. They come from five countries: the United States, Russia, China, France and Canada. No other crewed spacecraft is in orbit right now, so everyone in space is living on one of the two stations.

The number is higher than usual because the ISS is in the middle of a crew handover. NASA's SpaceX Crew-13 arrived on October 1, and the Crew-12 astronauts they are replacing are scheduled to undock no earlier than October 7. When they leave, the count will drop to 10. Below you will find every name, agency and mission, station by station.

Are there any other missions in space right now?

No. As of October 5, 2026 there are no short private missions, free-flying spacecraft or suborbital flights with people on board. When those happen, they add to the total for a few minutes (suborbital flights) or a few days to weeks (private orbital missions).

How Many Astronauts Are on the ISS?

Right now there are 11 people on the ISS. That is a temporary peak. During normal operations the station usually has a crew of 7: four who arrive on a SpaceX Crew Dragon and three who arrive on a Russian Soyuz. The count rises to 11 for a few days whenever a new Dragon crew arrives before the previous one leaves, so the outgoing astronauts can hand over their work.

Crew members usually stay about six months. Rotations are staggered, so the Dragon and Soyuz crews change at different times and the station is never left empty. The ISS has had people on board without interruption since 2 November 2000. To see where it is at this moment, check where the ISS is right now on the live map.

How Many People Are on Tiangong Now?

There are 3 people on Tiangong, which is its standard crew. China rotates crews roughly every six months with a new Shenzhou spacecraft. During each handover the new crew arrives before the old one leaves, so Tiangong briefly hosts 6 people.

Tiangong flies a little lower than the ISS and on a less tilted orbit, so it never travels as far north or south. Our guide to the Tiangong space station explains its modules, its orbit and how to see it from the ground.

Why Does the Number of People in Space Change?

The total moves up and down several times a year, for four main reasons:

  • Crew handovers. Both stations overlap old and new crews for a few days, so the count jumps and then falls back.
  • Short visits. Some missions bring guests for a week or two, such as private astronaut missions to the ISS or international visitors to Tiangong.
  • Suborbital flights. Tourist flights that briefly cross the edge of space add people to the count for only a few minutes.
  • Independent missions. Occasionally a crewed spacecraft flies on its own without docking to a station, which adds a separate group to the total.

When Will the Number Change Next?

Two changes are expected in the coming weeks:

  • Crew-12 returns to Earth. NASA and SpaceX are targeting undocking no earlier than October 7, 2026, followed by a splashdown off the coast of California. Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, Sophie Adenot and Andrey Fedyaev will come home, leaving 7 people on the ISS and 10 in space in total.
  • Shenzhou 24 launches to Tiangong. China's next crew mission is expected in late October or November 2026. It is expected to carry the first Pakistani astronaut for a visit of about a week, which would make him the first foreign astronaut to board Tiangong. Launch dates and crews are only confirmed by China shortly before each flight.

We update this page when crews change, so the count above always shows the date it was last checked.

What Is the Most People Ever in Space at Once?

The record is 20 people, set for a few minutes in May 2023, when six people on a suborbital flight joined the crews already in orbit on the ISS, including a private mission visiting the station, and on Tiangong. Counting only people in orbit, the totals have ranged in recent years from around 10 to the high teens, depending on how many visits overlap.

At the other end of the scale, there has not been a single moment without people in space since 31 October 2000, when the first long-term ISS crew launched. They boarded the station two days later. More than 780 people have now flown to space in total, from over 50 countries.

Can You See the Space Stations From Earth?

Yes. Both stations are easy to see with the naked eye when they pass over your area at dusk or dawn. The ISS is one of the brightest objects in the night sky: a steady white light that crosses the sky in a few minutes without blinking. Tiangong is fainter but still brighter than most stars.
